# Mira Machine — Simulated Beta Panel

Date: 2026-07-16
Build tested: `7dd0531`
Target: `http://127.0.0.1:4322/?ollama=1`

## Method

Six simulated personas across three independent agents actually played the running application. This is structured product testing, not evidence from recruited human participants.

Personas:

1. casual mobile puzzle player;
2. non-gamer science-fiction reader, age 45+;
3. experienced interactive-fiction/mystery player;
4. escape-room/evidence-board deduction player;
5. privacy/on-device-AI enthusiast;
6. accessibility-conscious product QA tester.

The panel used desktop and 390–393px mobile viewports, successful recipes, wrong/novel combinations, local Gemma 4, repeat pairs, restart, hide-used, keyboard navigation, and progression through Percy Comes Home. The project’s automated suite separately covers both authored chapters completely.

## Ratings

| Persona | Rating | Main reason |
|---|---|---|
| Casual mobile puzzle player | Fine | Enjoyed discovery but lost direction after several misses |
| Non-gamer sci-fi reader | Needs improvement | Loved story; pairing mechanic and lack of text input conflicted with chat expectations |
| IF/mystery player | Great | Strong hook, Mira voice, emotional escalation |
| Deduction puzzle player | Fine | Early deductions fair; midgame drifts toward brute force |
| On-device-AI enthusiast | Fine | Grounded, fast, repeatable AI; trust/status presentation is weak |
| Accessibility QA | Fine after verification | Real contrast/cognitive-load concerns; reported hidden-focus blocker did not reproduce |

## Great

### Mira is the strongest asset

Repeatedly praised lines included:

- “So Percy was not lost. He was choosing badly, or bravely.”
- “Small rover. Enormous stubbornness.”
- “Percy didn’t choose between orders. He chose between stories.”
- “He sent me good-morning pictures. I filed them as telemetry. I am not proud.”

Mira’s analytical surface, guilt, denial, and emotional cracks create a reason to continue beyond solving recipes.

### The opening works

“Percy stopped responding 47 hours ago. Mission control filed it as dust” establishes character, stakes, and contradiction immediately. Every panel understood the first combination.

### The core mystery escalates well

The move from missing rover → deliberate choice → Mira’s memory problem → living passenger was considered compelling. Testers wanted to continue because Percy’s choice changes how they understand Mira.

### Mobile mechanics are physically usable

At 390–393px:

- no page-level horizontal overflow;
- two-column vertical clue tray;
- clue buttons approximately 182–184 × 52px;
- controls remained responsive;
- no console errors.

### Local Gemma behavior is grounded

A tested novel pair returned in approximately 1.6 seconds and explicitly preserved uncertainty:

> “The looping nature of the Ghost Trail and the way the Prism Sample splits light suggest a pattern, but I lack any shared moment to connect them.”

Repeating the pair returned the same response without another model call. The model did not invent canonical story facts.

## Fine

### Wrong pairs are narratively coherent

They usually identify missing evidence rather than punishing the player. This is better than “invalid combination,” but many responses still sound structurally similar.

### The one/two-message cadence is better

Ordinary responses generally use one bubble and important discoveries use multiple beats. Testers did not report the previous always-two pattern as a blocker.

### The clue tray works, but accumulates cognitive load

The vertical grid fixes physical scrolling. It does not solve the mental problem of distinguishing:

- new evidence;
- side flavor;
- required evidence;
- exhausted evidence;
- evidence relevant to the current question.

### Local AI works but is not legible as a product feature

`Gemma 4 test` is accurate for the QA URL but looks disabled or unfinished. Production says `Local AI on`, yet there is no discreet explanation of what is local, what AI is allowed to do, or whether the last response came from authored logic or inference.

## Needs improvement

### 1. The action does not fully feel like chat

This was the strongest cross-persona design issue.

The screen looks like conversation, but the player cannot type. Tapping two fragments causes the game to author the player’s sentence. Non-gamers may wonder why a chat interface has no composer; experienced players may feel the game is speaking for them.

**Resolution:** introduce a compact evidence composer. Selecting two fragments creates a draft such as:

> “The signal says Percy stopped, but the tracks say he kept moving.”

The player explicitly sends it. A later version can allow editing or a short free-text question while still binding canonical recipes to the selected evidence.

### 2. Midgame deductions become brute-forceable

The first deduction is fair because Mira states the contradiction directly. Later, broad clue labels and many plausible semantic links make required recipes difficult to infer.

Example fairness snag:

- Dust Shroud: “hid the true route”
- Rover Tracks: “records the path”

Players reasonably expect the pair to matter, yet it produces a generic missing-link response.

**Resolution:** classify wrong pairs as:

- unrelated;
- right theme, missing bridge;
- contradiction that rules something out;
- evidence for an existing question.

For plausible near-misses, name the missing bridge specifically: “Route and shroud fit, but we still need proof the dust arrived after Percy turned.”

### 3. Progress and current intent are hard to retain

After several discoveries, players know individual facts but lose the active question. Side discoveries do not clearly communicate whether they are optional, useful now, or groundwork for later.

**Resolution:** add one compact, in-character “Mira is trying to understand…” line near the clue tray. It should update only after major discoveries and state a tension, not reveal a recipe.

### 4. New evidence is easy to lose in the inventory

New clues briefly animate but remain among every older fragment. Casual players want stronger confirmation and a way to locate what changed.

**Resolution:** scroll the new clue into view, retain a `new` marker until first selection, and optionally provide `New / All` filtering. Do not recommend an exact recipe.

### 5. Accessibility polish is incomplete

Verified concerns:

- fragment metadata is small and muted;
- supporting gold/gray text is borderline for low vision;
- no explicit reduced-motion stylesheet was demonstrated;
- icon-only restart needs strong focus treatment;
- the clue tray competes with chat for vertical space on small screens.

One panel reported focusable 0×0 hidden controls. Direct verification found no actionable zero-size elements and no hidden controls in the actual Tab sequence, so this is not accepted as a reproduced blocker.

The restart/filter report was also overstated: after restart, `show all` remains because hide-used is still enabled, but no starter fragment has yet been used, so all are correctly visible. The state is logically consistent but potentially confusing.

**Resolution:** improve metadata contrast and size, add `prefers-reduced-motion`, expose `aria-pressed` on hide-used, and decide explicitly whether restart resets the filter.

### 6. AI trust information needs a quieter, clearer home

Privacy-focused users want to know:

- model runs locally;
- authored logic decides truth;
- AI interprets novel pairs;
- repeated responses may be cached.

**Resolution:** use a small `Local AI on` status with a details sheet. Keep endpoint/model/cache diagnostics out of the main conversation.

## Prioritized resolution plan

### P0 — Conversation contract

1. Add evidence composer with explicit Send/Ask action.
2. Add one-line onboarding: “Choose two fragments to ask Mira what changes when they are read together.”
3. Do not add a blank generic chatbot input until free text has a defined effect on authored state.

### P1 — Deduction fairness

1. Audit every plausible near-miss.
2. Replace generic wrong-pair questions with diagnostic missing-bridge responses.
3. Add a compact current unresolved question.
4. Preserve no-recipe-hint rule.

### P1 — Inventory comprehension

1. Keep new marker until first use.
2. Scroll newly discovered evidence into view.
3. Consider `New / All`, not more category colors.

### P1 — Accessibility

1. Increase metadata contrast and minimum text size.
2. Add reduced-motion support.
3. Add explicit `aria-pressed`, labels, and focus-visible styling.
4. test at 320px, 200% zoom, keyboard-only, and screen reader landmarks.

### P2 — Local AI trust

1. Normalize QA and production status wording.
2. Add optional details sheet for local model/source/cache.
3. Keep canonical authority explanation concise.

## Product verdict

**Ship/no-ship for a closed beta: ship after P0 conversation-contract work.**

The story, character, mobile mechanics, and grounded local AI are strong enough for testing. The remaining blocker is not visual polish: it is whether the player understands what kind of conversation they are having and why tapping evidence is equivalent to speaking.
